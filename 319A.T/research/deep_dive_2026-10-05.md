## What this business is
Next Generation Technology Group buys Japanese manufacturing companies and keeps them. The customers are the industrial buyers of the subsidiaries, which make parts and equipment and, in one case, rent and sell used forklifts. The parent does not run a single factory. It buys the whole company, usually for cash, and leaves the local name in place. It then tries to raise cash generation with a playbook it calls NGP. The shares trade on the Tokyo Stock Exchange Growth market as 319A.

## Business & moat
The company calls itself a serial acquirer. It buys manufacturing businesses and a few businesses that serve factories. It says it does not buy turnarounds, it usually buys 100%, and it does not plan to sell what it buys. Financing is debt at the acquired company, with low interest and a long repayment. Consolidated net debt on June 30, 2026 was 5,336 million yen. The company defines that as total debt minus cash, long-term deposits, and investment securities. Against its own 4,000 million yen EBITDA forecast, that is 1.33 times, below the 3 to 4 times band it calls appropriate.

### Mental models

The useful picture is a roll-up that must keep finding sellers who care about the workforce and the name, not only the price. The incentive is the founder's 65.54% stake and the written policy of no dividend while acquisitions continue. Inversion: if the founder stops, or if banks stop lending at the subsidiary, the growth story stops and the multiple is the thing that was expensive. Circle of competence sits in Japanese small-company succession, not in a technology product.

### Business mechanics

Revenue is the sales of the owned factories. In the first half of 2026 those sales were 13,486 million yen, up 140% from 5,619 million yen. Adjusted EBITDA was 2,992 million yen, up from 1,026 million yen. The company says the jump came from the portfolio companies and from a full year of the seven businesses bought in fiscal 2025. It does not publish a separate organic growth rate. Adjusted EBITDA margin was 22.2% in the half, against 18.3% a year earlier. Operating cash flow was 1,779 million yen. Cash paid for new subsidiaries was 1,725 million yen. Two companies entered the group in the half, Horikoshi Seiki and Osaki Electric Clutch and Brake. Sanko Giken was agreed on July 1, 2026, after the balance-sheet date, and the price is not in that note.

### Moat

The claimed edge is that sellers prefer a buyer who will not resell, plus a pipeline the company counts at about 500 opportunities a year and 2,751 reviews since inception. That is a real sourcing story. It is not yet a proven competitive advantage. A durable advantage would show up as a repeated gap between the price paid and the cash the subsidiary then produces. The company says it uses a disciplined EBITDA multiple and does not print the number. Until that gap is visible deal by deal, the advantage stays unproven.

## Risks & inversion
**Primary risk:** the price capitalizes a deal pace the company has not put in its forecast. If that pace slows, the multiple compresses and the owner loses capital even if the factories keep earning.

- Founder dependence. The securities report says the business relies on Eiichi Arai and that his stake was 65.54% on the filing date.
- Thin trading. Free float was 29.06% at December 31, 2025, and the listing is the Growth market.
- Accounting noise. The half included a 450 million yen bargain-purchase gain and a jump in the directors' retirement provision from 755 million yen to 1,582 million yen. Adjusted profit removes the bargain gain. It does not remove a larger retirement provision if that provision is cash later.

If we are wrong and the company keeps buying at attractive prices for a decade, the base case is too harsh. The filing does not give the entry multiple, so that path stays outside the base.
