## What this business is

CrowdStrike Holdings sells cloud-native endpoint and identity security software on a subscription model. Customers pay recurring fees for the Falcon platform, which consolidates threat detection, response, and adjacent modules (identity, cloud workload, log management). Revenue reached $4.81 billion in fiscal 2026 (year ended January 31, 2026) per `CRWD/investor-documents/sec-edgar/10-K_20260305_rpt20260131_acc0001535527_26_000010.htm`. The company generates substantial cash from operations ($1.61 billion in fiscal 2026) while reporting a GAAP net loss ($162.5 million) because stock-based compensation and acquisition-related costs remain large relative to reported earnings.

## Why the market might be wrong

The market prices CrowdStrike as a premium-growth compounder after the July 2024 Falcon content-update incident, yet filing cash economics already support more than $1.3 billion of normalized owner earnings. Investors may overweight GAAP losses and headline churn fears while underweighting operating cash flow that grew 29% compounded over five years and net cash of roughly $4.0 billion against modest long-term debt. If module adoption and net retention stabilize post-incident, owner cash can grow faster than the screening-grade reinvestment template assumes; conversely, if incident-related churn or elevated sales and marketing persist, owner cash could compress toward the low-case bridge typed in the prospective falsifier.

## Business & moat

### Operating snapshot

CrowdStrike reports one operating segment: subscription software and professional services. Subscription revenue dominates the income statement; deferred revenue and remaining performance obligations indicate multi-year contracted demand. Fiscal 2026 revenue of $4.81 billion grew from $3.95 billion in fiscal 2025 (10-K FY2026). Operating cash flow was $1.61 billion versus capital spending of $302 million, yielding normalized owner earnings of $1.31 billion per the fact ledger bridge.

### Thesis pillars

1. **Platform consolidation:** Customers expand from endpoint protection into identity, cloud, and observability modules, raising switching costs when deployments integrate with Falcon APIs.
2. **Cash economics vs GAAP optics:** Operating cash flow compounded roughly 29% over five years while GAAP net income remained volatile and negative in fiscal 2026 because of non-cash compensation.
3. **Balance sheet optionality:** Cash and equivalents of $4.72 billion against $746 million long-term debt (10-Q April 2026) fund acquisitions (e.g., Pangea, Next) without near-term refinancing pressure.
4. **Incident overhang:** The July 2024 content-update outage created churn and reputational risk; subsequent filings must show net retention and sales efficiency stabilizing.
