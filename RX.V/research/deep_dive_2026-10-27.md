## What this business is

BioSyent Inc. is a Canadian specialty company whose common shares trade on the TSX Venture Exchange under the symbol RX. It commercializes medicines and related products, mostly in Canada, under licenses or after buying rights. The 2025 annual report describes three lines that the second-quarter 2026 management discussion later reports as Pharmaceutical, Oral Health, and a legacy insecticide business (Protect-It, through Hedley). Oral Health is Oral Science Inc., bought on 1 March 2026. The company says its model does not need heavy ongoing capital spending, and it returns cash through a normal course issuer bid and a quarterly dividend.

## Why the market might be wrong

The cash yield at today's price does not show a clear miss by the market. Trailing owner cash includes Oral Science only from 1 March 2026, so a full year of that business is not in the starting figure, and the statements do not split oral-health cash from operations on their own line. A neglected TSX Venture listing is possible. The cash the business has produced does not pay for that neglect at CAD 16.61.

## Executive summary

BioSyent sells specialty products in Canada, with a smaller international pharmaceutical line and a legacy grain-protectant product. It closed the Oral Science acquisition on 1 March 2026. The last completed TSX Venture close was CAD 16.61 on 26 October 2026. The base case annual return is **9.4%** per year over seven years. That is below a 15% hurdle, so the stance is watch. At this price the name is a pass for new capital. Starting owner cash is CAD 0.948921 per diluted share, from cash from operations in the twelve months ended 30 June 2026, after spending on property and equipment and on intangible assets. Growth of 6% then 3%, and a sale at 15 times year-7 owner cash, are judgments. They are not company guidance. Net cash is disclosed and is not added to the starting figure.

## Business & moat

BioSyent in-licenses or buys products, then sells them, mainly through wholesalers and pharmacy chains in Canada. The 2025 annual report places most licensors as unnamed European partners. Brand dollar sales are not disclosed. Growth rates are.

Canadian pharmaceutical sales were CAD 37,143,783 in 2025, 13% above CAD 32,931,149 in 2024, which was 11% above 2023. That was 86% of 2025 sales. International pharmaceutical sales were CAD 3,735,959 (9%), including Tibelia international sales of CAD 2,389,277. Legacy Protect-It sales were CAD 2,172,241 (5%), against CAD 1,169,773 in 2024. Total sales were CAD 43,051,983 in 2025, against CAD 35,030,897 in 2024. The management discussion states a 17% compound growth rate in total sales from 2023 to 2025. Exact 2023 sales dollars were not extracted here.

Products named in the 2025 annual report include FeraMAX oral iron (Therapeutic 150, Powder 15, Maintenance 45), Cathejell (in-licensed 2009, launched 2012), RepaGyn (2013, launched 2014), Proktis-M (2014), Tibella (in-licensed 2016, launched in Canada in 2020; worldwide rights bought from Novalon SA in September 2024), Combogesic (in-licensed 2019, launched 2020), Inofolic (license and supply in 2020, launched August 2023), and Gelclair (distribution from 2022). In September 2025 the company agreed to return Canadian Combogesic rights and stopped distributing it in January 2026. The second-quarter 2026 management discussion says Health Canada approved Thyconvi (levothyroxine sodium oral solution) on 19 May 2026 under an exclusive Canadian license, and that a launch was being prepared. The same discussion says the FeraMAX brand was named the number one recommended oral iron in Canada for an eleventh year. It also says Canadian oral iron faced increasing competitive activity, with FeraMAX sales up 5% in the second quarter of 2026 and 8% in the first half, and Tibella up 9% and 15% in those same periods.

Second-quarter 2026 external revenue was CAD 11,110,365 pharmaceutical, CAD 8,224,402 oral health, and CAD 537,996 other (insecticide). For the six months, those lines were CAD 21,913,472, CAD 11,209,375, and CAD 658,098. Total revenue was CAD 19,872,763 in the quarter and CAD 33,780,945 in the half. Gross profit was CAD 13,969,817 in the quarter and CAD 24,138,053 in the half. Cost of goods rose to 29% of sales in the first half of 2026 from 23% in the first half of 2025, which the management discussion ties to the oral-health mix.

Full-year 2025 gross profit was CAD 32,963,028. The company states that as 77% of sales (the dollars are 76.6% of CAD 43,051,983). In 2024 gross profit was CAD 27,856,073, stated as 80% of sales. The company's own earnings figure before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortization was CAD 12,122,756 in 2025, CAD 9,343,012 in 2024, and CAD 7,926,478 in 2023. Net income after tax was CAD 9,012,232 in 2025, CAD 7,270,104 in 2024, and CAD 6,460,127 in 2023. Operating profit was CAD 11,192,911 in 2025 against CAD 8,753,064 in 2024. Diluted earnings per share were CAD 0.783 in 2025 on a diluted weighted average of 11,506,606 shares.

Cash from operations was CAD 9,009,146 in 2025 and CAD 8,663,484 in 2024. Property and equipment additions were CAD 54,044 in 2025. Net intangible additions were CAD 335,764 in 2025, against CAD 4,627,369 in 2024 when the Tibelia rights were bought. First-half 2026 cash from operations was CAD 5,247,905, against CAD 2,752,722 in the first half of 2025. The management discussion says the 2026 half includes oral-health cash but does not publish a separate oral-health cash-from-operations number.

Customer concentration is disclosed on receivables, not as a named share of revenue. At 31 December 2025 one customer was 40% of net trade receivables (49% in 2024), with three others at 15%, 13%, and 12%. At 30 June 2026 the four figures were 30%, 15%, 11%, and 8%. International FeraMAX and Tibelia depend on distribution partners. The 2025 management discussion flags geopolitical uncertainty for Middle East FeraMAX shipments. Raw materials can sit with a single supplier. That is a filing risk statement, not a named supplier list.

René C. Goehrum is Chairman, President, and Chief Executive Officer in the 2025 annual report. A filing percent for management and director ownership was not in the statements read here. The company investor page is marketing and is not used as the ownership source.

The moat is unproven. Repeat pharmacist and physician recommendation of FeraMAX is a commercial fact the company reports. It is not a license that blocks rivals, and the company itself reports more competition in Canadian oral iron. Combogesic shows a license can be given back. Oral Science adds a second profit pool and also a lower gross profit mix, goodwill of CAD 3,796,739, and an earn-out.

## Payoff & return

The payoff is owner cash from the operating business, not a dated asset sale and not a sum of parts. Downside is not floored by a hard asset. Net cash after the term loan and the contingent consideration is about CAD 6.4 million at 30 June 2026 (CAD 12,236,790 minus CAD 2,000,000 minus CAD 3,845,837), roughly CAD 0.55 per diluted share on the second-quarter diluted share count. That cash is upside the model does not grow or multiply. Leases stay in operations. Because the bear case is not a bounded asset value, the downside test is only partly met. The base annual return is under 15%, and the advantage is unproven, so the stance is watch.

## Risks & inversion

**Primary risk:** A product right can be returned. Combogesic's Canadian rights were handed back under a September 2025 transition agreement, and distribution stopped in January 2026. The same class of risk sits on FeraMAX, Tibella, and the newer oral-health brands if a partner or a competitor changes the terms of trade.

Other risks, from the filings:

- Gross profit has already compressed. Cost of goods was 23% of sales in 2025 against 20% in 2024, and 29% of sales in the first half of 2026 against 23% a year earlier, after Oral Science.
- Receivables sit with a few wholesalers. One customer was 40% of net trade receivables at 31 December 2025 and 30% at 30 June 2026.
- International sales depend on partners and, for Middle East FeraMAX, on logistics the company says are uncertain.
- The Oral Science earn-out and the royalty cap can take cash in later years. The June 2026 estimate of contingent consideration is CAD 3,845,837. The royalty ceiling is CAD 6,000,000. Timing is not scheduled in the base case.
- The term loan was still CAD 2,000,000 at 30 June 2026, with a final principal date of 30 September 2026 in the facility note. A renewal after that date was not in the documents read here.
- Share count in the model is the diluted weighted average for the three months ended 30 June 2026 (11,629,144), not the 11,340,335 shares outstanding that day and not a twelve-month average. If the company keeps buying shares, owner cash per share can be higher than the model. If it issues shares for another deal, it can be lower.
- Thyconvi can help or can cost launch money. Neither is in the base growth rate.

What confirms a pass at CAD 16.61: oral-health cash does not stay at the acquired run rate, FeraMAX growth stalls, another license is returned, or the price stays near 17.5 times trailing owner cash. What would reopen the work: a price at which the same owner cash supports at least 15% a year, or a later filing that shows durable oral-health cash above the four months already counted, with gross profit no longer falling.

#### IRR arithmetic (show your work)

How we calculated the annual return. Inputs are price CAD 16.61 and starting owner cash per share CAD 0.948921. Years 1 through 5 grow at 6%. Years 6 and 7 grow at 3%. Year 7 also adds 15 times that year's owner cash. Figures per share, from the same cash-flow function the valuation tool uses:

1. Price paid today: CAD 16.61.
2. Year 1 owner cash: 0.948921 times 1.06 = CAD 1.005856.
3. Year 2: CAD 1.066208.
4. Year 3: CAD 1.130180.
5. Year 4: CAD 1.197991.
6. Year 5: CAD 1.269870.
7. Year 6, now at 3%: CAD 1.307966.
8. Year 7 owner cash: CAD 1.347205. Cash received in year 7 is that amount plus 15 times that amount, CAD 21.555287.
9. The valuation tool solves the annual return that sets the present value of this stream equal to the price. That return is 9.4% per year. The bear stream (flat cash, 12 times) is 1.4% per year. The bull stream (10% then 4%, 16 times) is 13.5% per year.

**Returns statement:** At CAD 16.61 per share, the base case is **9.4%** per year over seven years on starting owner cash of CAD 0.948921 per share.
