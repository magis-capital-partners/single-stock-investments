II.In our view, Microsoft’s existing climate-related disclosures are not a substitute for the robust, forward-looking information requested by the proposal. Such disclosure could allow investors to better evaluate the company’s success in meeting its 2030 targets.

 

Six years ago, Microsoft announced that by 2030, it would: 1) reduce its operational GHG emissions to near zero; 2) reduce its Scope 3 emissions by more than 50%; and 3) become carbon negative (i.e., remove more carbon dioxide than it emitted) across all scopes of its emissions. However, by the end of its fiscal year 2025, Microsoft’s operational and value chain emissions had exceeded its FY2020 baseline by 62%, attributable primarily to the growth of its AI business.23 With the company’s emissions rising well beyond its established targets, it is reasonable for investors to probe more deeply into Microsoft’s plans, strategies, and contingencies related to meeting its targets.

 

Yet Microsoft asserts that its existing climate-related disclosures – both voluntary and required by law – already provide shareholders with “extensive decision-useful information.” However, we believe that investors, rather than companies themselves, are best positioned to determine the information they need to fully assess a company’s climate risk. In fact, for many years, investors have urged companies to provide additional disclosure through shareholder proposals or private dialogue, including requests to provide sustainability reports, operational and value chain GHG emissions, Taskforce on Climate-Related Financial Disclosures and CDP (formerly Carbon Disclosure Project) reporting, GHG reduction targets, water stewardship activities, deforestation risk, and climate transition plans.

 

This call for greater transparency comes at a critical moment. With four years left to reach its 2030 targets, Microsoft has a narrow window during which to secure additional clean energy supply and drive down emissions across its organization and value chain. While timelines for the company’s critical energy and emissions reduction projects may shift, it is wholly implausible that Microsoft is ignorant of its path toward reaching its targets - and whether it is likely to hit them. Although the company’s 2026 Environmental Sustainability report discusses progress the company is making and initiatives it’s working on, we believe its reporting does not supplant the forward-looking information and analysis contemplated by the proposal. Microsoft could offer more detail to its investors, who may seek greater visibility into how the company is executing its climate-related plans and strategies, particularly when they have significant impacts on its business.

Examples of meaningful disclosure may include, but are not limited to, the following list (Footnotes contain links to relevant disclosures):

 

·Disclosure according to the Transition Plan Taskforce (TPT) Disclosure Framework - The TPT Disclosure Framework provides companies guidance on reporting valuable climate transition information – including their goals and targets as well as elements to achieve them such as emissions reduction pathway dependencies, key assumptions, milestones, short-, medium- and long-term action steps, and anticipated capex and opex. A comprehensive climate transition plan explains to shareholders, customers, and stakeholders how a company intends to adapt its business to meeting its climate targets and while reducing its climate risk.24,25
·Contingency plan – Disclosure of a contingency plan could help answer an important question: What alternatives is Microsoft considering to reach its 2030 targets in the face of increasing supply chain and data center-related emissions? How is the company prepared to adapt?26
·Stress test – Microsoft could stress test its strategies for achieving its 2030 targets. A stress test would analyze factors under the company’s control, such as its capital expenditures, as well as factors out of its control such as material costs, technology readiness, or grid interconnection delays. The results would indicate its confidence in delivering on its various initiatives.27
·Anticipated emissions pathway to 2030 - Because Microsoft’s emissions have risen significantly in the last five years, publishing its anticipated emissions reduction trajectory compared with the IPCC’s low- and no-overshoot 1.5 degrees Celsius-aligned pathways28 could help investors understand how the company envisions its path forward and whether it will overshoot the IPCC’s recommended pathways temporarily or for the medium-term.29,30,31,32,33
·Enabled emissions – Microsoft sells AI software to the fossil fuel industry to facilitate oil and gas extraction.34 To maintain its position as a credible climate leader, Microsoft should detail efforts to reduce emissions enabled by its AI software that is tailored for use by the fossil fuel industry.
·Hourly clean energy matching data – In 2021, Microsoft set a 2030 target to hourly match 100% of its electricity consumption with clean energy. The company should disclose hourly clean energy matching data and explain why its target is no longer included in its sustainability reporting.

III.Falling short of its climate targets may create risk to Microsoft’s credibility, reputation, brand value, and ability to retain top talent, supporting the need for greater disclosure

 

Credibility and reputational strength can influence investor trust in companies.35 Some studies demonstrate that reputation and higher management credibility, as measured by, among other things, transparency policies36 and forecast accuracy on company targets and metrics37,38 can lead to easier access to and lower cost of capital.39,40 Underpinning investors’ abilities to assess a company’s credibility and determine reputational strength is transparency, which is why disclosure of Microsoft’s anticipated next steps, contingencies, and strategies for hitting its 2030 targets – even in the face of the complexities it has expressed - could deliver investor-useful information.

 

Microsoft has spent billions developing its AI capabilities and building and leasing millions of square feet of data centers,41 which it has financed through operating cash flow, sizeable debt issuance, and tax incentives.42,43 While the company is among the Magnificent 7, its ability to execute on plans to monetize its AI products and services is an area of heightened investor scrutiny with regular speculation about the likelihood of its success.44,45,46 Similarly, we believe that Microsoft’s ability to execute on its climate targets deserves heightened scrutiny, as it could be an indicator of management’s facility in meeting its overarching corporate goals.

IV.Leading corporate climate transition plans can serve as model disclosures for Microsoft

 

Through their public-facing climate transition plans, other companies are providing the types of disclosures that this proposal requests of Microsoft. For example:

 

·National Grid publishes scenario analyses to guide its “strategic decision-making process and support the delivery of [its] climate targets.”61 It describes the assumptions, dependencies and uncertainties underpinning its plan to reach its near-term science-based and its 2050 near-zero emissions reduction targets. Additionally, it outlines its near-term action steps and compares its anticipated high- and low-emissions reduction pathways to a 1.5°C scenario pathway.62
·Ball Corporation provides a comprehensive set of scenario analyses describing factors that could impact its ability to reach its net-zero by 2040 emissions reduction target. Importantly, it outlines alternative strategies for achieving its target in case its pathway dependencies change. 63,64 It also stress tests the robustness of each of its emissions reduction strategies and reports the results of its analysis through a heat map.65
·BHP set near-term and net-zero targets for its operational GHG emissions and publishes its anticipated emissions reduction pathways to 2030 and 2050, respectively. Danone66,67 set near-term science-based and 2050 net-zero emissions reduction targets and publishes its anticipated emissions reduction pathways to 2030 compared to a 1.5°C scenario pathway.68 Note: Microsoft published an emissions reduction pathway for its Scopes 1- 3 emissions in 2020, but has not subsequently.69

V.Climate transition plan disclosure may prove valuable to universal owners who view climate change as a systemic risk

 

Climate transition plan disclosure may be valuable to universal owners who view climate change as a systemic risk. Given that the growth and stability of the economy is a key driver of long-term portfolio returns for diversified investors, climate change is an important consideration, beyond its impact on Microsoft itself. 

 

Due to the current and predicted wide-ranging impacts of climate change, it is considered by many as a significant macroeconomic threat.70,71 In fact, one study shows that the nominal returns of an average portfolio comprising 60% equities and 40% bonds could be 10-40% lower over the next 40 years due to climate change impacts, relative to a baseline without additional climate impacts.72 Further, a 2024 study by the EDHEC-Risk Climate Impact Institute has introduced a new model demonstrating there is a heightened risk of downward revisions to global equity valuations if the pace of decarbonization does not accelerate. It states that “[t]he difference in equity valuations between a no-climate-damage world and a world with climate damages can be significant, ranging from less than 10% if prompt and robust abatement action is taken, rising to more than 40% in a close-to-no-action case.”73

 

Enhanced climate-related disclosure can benefit universal holders and broadly diversified investors by improving their ability to assess system-wide financial risks that climate change poses across portfolios, sectors, and the real economy. For investors whose long-term returns depend on overall economic health, greater transparency on climate risks helps identify externalities—such as potential physical damage and transition risk—that can reduce aggregate market returns if left unmanaged. Within this context, it is important for investors to understand how individual companies are acting to increase or decrease risks to their wider portfolios. Uneven reporting or underreporting of investor-useful information by companies may thwart investors’ abilities to fully assess these risks.

VI.Responses to specific claims made in Microsoft’s opposition statement

 

Microsoft contends that implementing the proposal would require a “separate, stand-alone report.” That assertion is incorrect. Nowhere does the proposal dictate where Microsoft should report the requested information. The company also claims that the proposal would require it to “[focus] on speculative future outcomes.” Yet Microsoft regularly publishes projections and predictions such as short- and long-term guidance, detailed revenue projections, anticipated operational and capital expenditures, and earnings compound annual growth rate.74 In its publications, including its 2026 Environmental Sustainability, it includes the caveat that its predictions and projections, i.e. its forward-looking statements, may materially differ from actual outcomes. We believe that Microsoft could and should apply the same caveats to the information requested by the proposal.

 

VII.Conclusion

 

Without additional disclosure, it may prove difficult to gauge how successful Microsoft will be in reaching its 2030 GHG reduction targets. Greater transparency could prepare investors for any significant strategic pivots the company might have to make to fulfill its commitments, and it could also help investors understand whether Microsoft is at risk of diminished credibility, reputational or brand damage, limited ability to attract top talent or simply a lack of investor confidence should it fall well short of its targets.
