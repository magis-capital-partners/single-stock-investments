Item 7.01Regulation FD Disclosure.

 

United Airlines, Inc. (“United”), a wholly-owned subsidiary of United Airlines Holdings, Inc. (“UAL” and, together with United, the “Company”), continues to see a significant impact in demand for air travel. In the last two weeks, ending September 7, 2020, there has been a moderate improvement in bookings for leisure travel in the domestic United States and certain short-haul destinations in Latin America and the Caribbean.

 

The Company does not currently expect the recovery from COVID-19 to follow a linear path. As such, the Company’s actual flown capacity may differ materially from its currently scheduled capacity. The Company’s scheduled capacity for the third quarter of 2020 is now expected to decrease approximately 70% year-over-year, as compared to the Company’s previous guidance of a decrease of approximately 65% year-over-year. Accordingly, the Company now expects passenger revenue to be down by approximately 85% in the third quarter of 2020 as compared to the third quarter of 2019, compared to the Company’s previous guidance of a decrease of approximately 83% year-over-year.

 

The Company plans to continue to proactively evaluate and cancel flights on a rolling 60-day basis until it sees signs of a recovery in demand, and expects demand to remain suppressed and plateau at levels of around 50%, relative to 2019 levels, until a widely accepted treatment and/or vaccine for COVID-19 is widely available.

 

The Company continues to expect average daily cash burn during the third quarter of 2020 to be approximately $25 million per day. For this purpose, “cash burn” is defined as net cash from operations, less investing and financing activities. Proceeds from the issuance of new debt (including any borrowings under the Coronavirus Aid, Relief, and Economic Security Act (the “CARES Act”)1 but excluding expected aircraft financing), government grants associated with the Payroll Support Program of the CARES Act, issuance of new UAL common stock, net proceeds from sale of short-term and other investments and changes in restricted cash balances are not included in this figure. The Company also continues to expect total available liquidity, which consists of cash and cash equivalents, the Company’s remaining capacity under its revolving credit facility, and funds available pursuant to the Loan Program under the CARES Act, to be over $18 billion at the end of the third quarter of 2020.

 

The information in this Item 7.01 is being furnished and shall not be deemed to be “filed” for purposes of Section 18 of the Securities Exchange Act of 1934, as amended (the “Exchange Act”), or otherwise subject to the liabilities of that Section, and shall not be deemed incorporated by reference into any registration statement or other document filed pursuant to the Securities Act of 1933, as amended, except as shall be expressly set forth by specific reference in such filing.
