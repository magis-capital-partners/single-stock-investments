Bloomberg—Paulson-Backed NovaGold Strikes Deal for Full Ownership of Alaska Mine

 

By Jacob Lorinc and Sybilla Gross

 

July 22, 2026

 

·NovaGold Resources Inc. struck a deal to obtain full ownership of the Donlin Gold project in Alaska, creating a US-based company called NovaGold Corp. valued at about $4.2 billion.
·The mine would produce about 1.1 million ounces of gold annually over nearly three decades, with construction costs estimated at more than $9 billion.
·The new ownership structure will give the company easier access to financing sources like government agencies, sovereign wealth funds and private investors, according to the company.

 

NovaGold Resources Inc., a company backed by hedge-fund billionaire John Paulson, struck a deal to obtain full ownership of the Donlin Gold project in Alaska in a move to control one of the largest undeveloped gold mines.

 

The all-stock transaction will create a US-based company called NovaGold Corp. valued at about $4.2 billion. Existing shareholders would own 65% of the new company, while Paulson would receive 35%. Including his existing NovaGold shares, Paulson will own about 40% of the company’s economic interest, according to a statement Wednesday.

 

Shares of NovaGold jumped 12% in New York on Wednesday.

 

The deal marks the latest effort to build an expensive project in Alaska that could become the largest gold mine in the US. With construction costs estimated at more than $9 billion, the mine would produce about 1.1 million ounces of gold annually over nearly three decades, according to the company.

 

Companies across the mining sector are taking a fresh look at stalled development projects after gold surged to record highs this year, boosting expected returns from new mines. The Trump administration has also sought to accelerate domestic mining through faster permitting and federal financing initiatives designed to reduce US reliance on overseas mineral supplies.

 

NovaGold said that the new ownership structure — which will see it redomiciled to the US and co-chaired by Paulson — will give the company easier access to financing sources like government agencies, sovereign wealth funds and private investors.

 

“The administration is very focused on, rather than buying natural resources abroad, produce them domestically, and they want to facilitate and support domestic production of minerals including gold,” Paulson said in an interview.

 

Read More: John Paulson Gets a Gold Mine in US Critical Minerals Scramble

 

Paulson, the hedge fund manager who made billions betting against the US housing market before the 2008 financial crisis, has become one of the mining industry’s most prominent investors over the past decade, amassing large stakes in gold companies and projects.

 

Several of his companies have received backing from the Trump administration, including Perpetua Resources Corp., which was designated a priority project by the White House, and Trilogy Metals Inc., an Alaska-focused mining company that received an investment from the US government in October before Paulson sold his shares.

 

Located in the wilderness of southwest Alaska, Donlin has long been considered one of the mining industry’s crown jewels. The deposit contains nearly 40 million ounces of gold resources — enough to rank among the world’s largest undeveloped gold projects — but the enormous cost of building the mine has repeatedly delayed construction. Barrick Mining Corp. owned the site for years before selling it to NovaGold last year.

 

 

 

 

“It’s a big number, but it’s a huge asset,” said Paulson, referring to the estimated $9 billion cost of developing Donlin. “The value it will create once it becomes a producing mine makes it very, very economic.”

 

The company has tapped two financial advisers to help with securing funding, he added, declining to specify names.

 

“We have enough preliminary information and enough engineering work that’s already been done that we can start these discussions now and start to outline what the potential sources of financing will be,” he said.
