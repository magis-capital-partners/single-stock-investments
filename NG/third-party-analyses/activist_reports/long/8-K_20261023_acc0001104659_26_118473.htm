Item 8.01 Other Events.

 

As previously disclosed, on July 21, 2026, NOVAGOLD Resources Inc. (“NOVAGOLD” or the “Company”) entered into a series of definitive transaction agreements in connection with a proposed transaction pursuant to which, subject to the satisfaction of certain closing conditions and upon the consummation thereof, NovaGold Corporation, a newly incorporated Delaware company (“New NOVAGOLD”), would become the ultimate parent of NOVAGOLD and its subsidiaries and would acquire the 40% interest in Donlin Gold LLC (“Donlin Gold”) held by Paulson Advisers LLC and certain of its affiliates (collectively, “Paulson”) in an all-share transaction, increasing NOVAGOLD’s ownership in Donlin Gold from 60% to 100% (the “Transactions”). Upon the consummation of the Transactions, current NOVAGOLD shareholders (inclusive of Paulson’s equity interest) would own approximately 65% of New NOVAGOLD and, inclusive of its existing equity ownership in NOVAGOLD, Paulson would own approximately 39.7% of the economic interest in New NOVAGOLD, while its voting interest would be capped at 19.99%.

 

On September 11, 2026, the Company filed a management information circular and preliminary proxy statement on Schedule 14A (the “Preliminary Circular and Proxy Statement”) with the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (the “SEC”) and applicable Canadian securities regulators in connection with the special meeting of the Company’s shareholders to be held on November 3, 2026, at which the Company’s shareholders will vote on the Transactions and other matters related thereto. On October 5, 2026, the Company filed with the SEC and applicable Canadian securities regulators a management information circular and definitive proxy statement on Schedule 14A (the “Circular and Proxy Statement”). Following the filing of the Circular and Proxy Statement, the Company commenced mailing of the Circular and Proxy Statement to its shareholders on or about October 5, 2026.

 

Since the filing of the Preliminary Circular and Proxy Statement, the Company has received certain ordinary course demand letters from counsel representing purported shareholders of the Company, generally alleging omissions or misstatements in the disclosures in the Preliminary Circular and Proxy Statement and the Circular and Proxy Statement and requesting that the Company file corrective disclosures prior to the special meeting of the Company’s shareholders (the demand letters are collectively referred to as the “Shareholder Letters”).

 

The Company denies that it has violated any laws or breached any duties to the Company’s shareholders, denies all allegations in the Shareholder Letters, and believes that no supplemental disclosure to the Circular and Proxy Statement was or is required under any applicable law, rule or regulation. However, solely to eliminate the burden and expense of potential litigation, to moot the disclosure claims made in the Shareholder Letters, to avoid nuisance and potential delay or disruption to the Transactions, and to provide additional information to the Company’s shareholders, the Company has determined to voluntarily supplement the Circular and Proxy Statement with the below disclosures. The Company believes that the disclosures in the Circular and Proxy Statement comply fully with applicable law and nothing in the below supplemental disclosures shall be deemed an admission of the legal merit, necessity or materiality under applicable law of any of the disclosures set forth herein or in the Circular and Proxy Statement.

1.The disclosure under the heading “The Arrangement and Related Transactions – Background of the Transaction” is hereby amended by amending and restating in its entirety the first full paragraph on page 45 of the Circular and Proxy Statement:

 

On September 7, 2025, the NOVAGOLD Board and members of management met virtually with representatives of Skadden, Blakes, NOVAGOLD’s Canadian counsel and Citi to discuss the September 4 Proposal, the Board’s fiduciary duties and potential conflicts of interest related to such proposal and the potential transactions contemplated thereby. At that meeting, the Board formed a transaction committee composed of certain directors, namely, Mr. Kalidas Madhavpeddi, Mr. Daniel Muñiz Quintanilla, Ms. Dawn Whittaker and Mr. Ali Erfan (the “Transaction Committee”), to work with the Company’s management and advisors to evaluate, negotiate or supervise negotiations on, and make recommendations to the full Board regarding the proposed transaction. That said, the Transaction Committee did not have authority to bind the Company with respect to any such proposal absent Board approval. The formation of the Transaction Committee was not prompted by actual or potential conflicts of interest. The Transaction Committee was formed for operational purposes and to ultimately report its findings and recommendations to the Board. The Transaction Committee’s duties included (i) retaining legal and financial advisors and settling and approving the terms of their engagements; (ii) working with management and the Company’s legal and financial advisors to review, consider and assess the September 4 Proposal and the proposed transaction; (iii) to the extent necessary, negotiating, or supervising negotiations by senior management of, the terms of the proposed transaction and related agreements or documents; (iv) reporting to the Board on its activities and recommendations; and (v) performing such other duties as might be assigned by the Board.

 

2.The disclosure under the heading “The Arrangement and Related Transactions – Background of the Transaction” is hereby amended by amending and restating in its entirety the second paragraph on page 46 of the Circular and Proxy Statement:

 

On October 10, 2025, NOVAGOLD and Paulson executed the Paulson Confidentiality Agreement, which did not contain a “don’t ask-don’t waive” standstill provision prohibiting Paulson from requesting that the Board or the Company release it from its standstill obligations. Later that day, representatives of NOVAGOLD (including certain members of the Transaction Committee, Dr. Kaplan and Mr. Lang), representatives of Paulson (including Mr. Paulson), and representatives of Skadden, Blakes, Citi, Kleinberg Kaplan and Goodmans met in person at Skadden’s New York offices (with certain attendees participating virtually) to discuss the September 4 Proposal. At the meeting, NOVAGOLD presented its counterproposal to the September 4 Proposal (the “October 10 Proposal”). In the October 10 Proposal, NOVAGOLD proposed a 30% discount to Paulson’s implied Donlin Gold valuation that would be used for pricing in the sale of the Donlin Gold interests, while acknowledging that there were limited historical precedents for similar transactions. The October 10 Proposal also included revised governance terms, including with respect to proposed board composition, voting rights, lock-up, transfer and standstill restrictions. At the meeting, the parties discussed the October 10 Proposal and following the meeting, Paulson sent NOVAGOLD a list of follow-up questions seeking clarification on certain matters discussed at the meeting.

 

3.The disclosure under the heading “The Arrangement and Related Transactions – Background of the Transaction” is hereby amended by amending and restating in its entirety the second full paragraph on page 47 of the Circular and Proxy Statement:

 

On October 29, 2025, the Board met virtually, with members of management and representatives of Citi, Skadden and Blakes. The Board discussed the strategic rationale, feasibility and risks of the Three-Party Transaction, including diligence on Party A, the risk of topping bidders, and the importance of shareholder reaction and market perception in an all-stock transaction. After discussion of certain legal considerations with the Board, Blakes recommended forming a special committee of fully independent directors to evaluate such transaction accordingly. After discussion, the Board unanimously approved resolutions, with Dr. Kaplan and Mr. Erfan abstaining from voting due to their interests in Electrum, to dissolve the Transaction Committee as its mandate was no longer required, and establish a special committee composed of independent and disinterested directors, due to certain overlapping shareholder interests in connection with the Three-Party Transaction (the “Special Committee”). The Special Committee, composed of Ms. Whittaker, Mr. Madhavpeddi and Mr. Kevin McArthur, was formed with a mandate to (i) examine the merits and fairness of review and make recommendations regarding the Three-Party Transaction and any potential alternatives (the “Special Committee”).; (ii) advise the Board as to whether the Three-Party Transaction was in the best interests of the Company; (iii) negotiate or supervise the negotiation of terms and agreements; (iv) discharge its mandate in coordination with senior management, directors and other advisors; (v) oversee and assist with obtaining approvals and preparing disclosure; (vi) supervise the preparation of a fairness opinion or formal valuation; (vii) report to the Board on its activities and recommendations; and (viii) perform such other duties as might be assigned by the Board. In carrying out its mandate, the Special Committee was permitted to engage, at the Company’s expense, financial advisors, valuators, legal counsel and other professional advisors; request that management assist the Special Committee and its advisors and provide them with information; and request that management take actions on behalf of the Company that the Special Committee considered necessary or advisable. The Special Committee was empowered to advise the Board on whether to veto the Three-Party Transaction or pursue an alternative course of action, but did not have authority to bind the Company with respect to any transaction or other value enhancement initiative absent Board Approval. Citi then provided an overview of the proposed Three-Party Transaction structure, including the acquisition of Paulson’s 40% interest in Donlin Gold by NOVAGOLD and a stock-for-stock merger with Party A, and the Board concluded that, while significant work remained on sequencing, structure and valuation, the framework was conceptually possible and provided a basis for the Special Committee to continue exploratory discussions.

4.The disclosure under the heading “The Arrangement and Related Transactions – Background of the Transaction” is hereby amended by amending and restating in its entirety the fourth full paragraph on page 48 of the Circular and Proxy Statement:

 

On December 6, 2025, NOVAGOLD and Party A executed the Party A Confidentiality Agreement, which did not contain a “don’t ask-don’t waive” standstill provision prohibiting Party A from requesting that the Board or the Company release it from its standstill obligations. Also on December 6, 2025, NOVAGOLD and Paulson sent a joint letter to Party A’s board expressing a non-binding interest in exploring the Three-Party Transaction (the “December 6 Letter”). The letter proposed a “merger of equals” style transaction based on current market values, with the combined company to be a newly created parent U.S. public corporation.

 

5.The disclosure under the heading “The Arrangement and Related Transactions – Background of the Transaction” is hereby amended by amending and restating in its entirety the first paragraph on page 49 of the Circular and Proxy Statement:

 

On February 12, 2026, the Board met virtually, with NOVAGOLD management and representatives of Citi, Skadden and Blakes, to discuss the February 9 Proposal. The Board expressed general support for further exploring the February 9 Proposal. Citi discussed the specifics of the February 9 Proposal, including the equity value of Donlin Gold and the accretive value to shareholders, noting that pro forma, Paulson would own approximately 42%, Electrum would own 14% and public shareholders would own approximately 44% of the issued and outstanding shares of the new combined company following closing based on the then current NOVAGOLD stock price and Paulson’s proposal. The Board unanimously resolved to: (i) dissolve the Special Committee previously constituted in connection with the Three-Party Transaction, discussions with respect to which had terminated on or about January 10, 2026, as its mandate was no longer required; (ii) authorize the Company to restart negotiations with Paulson with respect to the two-way transaction (“Project Phoenix”); and (iii) establish a Project Phoenix transaction committee comprising Ms. Whittaker, Mr. Madhavpeddi and Mr. McArthur to, among other things, assist NOVAGOLD management in negotiating the terms of Project Phoenix (the “Phoenix Transaction Committee”). The Board agreed that the Phoenix Transaction Committee should be comprised of the same members from the previously constituted Special Committee given their familiarity with certain aspects of the Three-Party Transaction that may continue to be relevant in connection with Project Phoenix.
