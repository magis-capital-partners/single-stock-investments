Ancora Alternatives LLC (“Ancora Alternatives”), together with the other participants named herein, have filed a definitive proxy statement and accompanying BLUE proxy card with the Securities and Exchange Commission (the “SEC”) to be used to solicit proxies for the election of their slate of director nominees at the 2024 annual meeting of shareholders of Norfolk Southern Corporation, a Virginia corporation (the “Company”). From time to time, Ancora Alternatives and the other participants named herein may refer shareholders of the Company to such materials reproduced herein, recent articles or other materials. The materials disclosed herein may be posted to the Ancora Alternatives campaign website at www.movenscforward.com.

 

On May 5, 2024, Jim Chadwick, President of Ancora Alternatives, sent the following letter to Chair Amy Miles of the board of directors of the Company.

 

 

May 5, 2024

 

Norfolk Southern Corporation

650 West Peachtree Street, NW

Atlanta, Georgia 30308

Attn: Amy Miles, Chair of Board

 

Subject: Incumbent Director Mongeau Should not Serve on the Norfolk Southern Board

 

Dear Chair Miles,

 

We recently saw reports that the U.S. Department of Justice launched an investigation into TD Bank in connection with a money laundering scheme involving hundreds of millions of dollars from illicit narcotics. Given Norfolk Southern Director Claude Mongeau’s membership on the TD Bank board, we have looked closely at other companies where he has served as a director. While we have no reason to believe that Mr. Mongeau is or ever has been involved in any wrongdoing, we see an unfortunate pattern that certain of those companies have had significant regulatory and compliance problems, as detailed below.

 

Norfolk Southern currently operates under heightened scrutiny in an already heavily regulated sector. We do not believe it is appropriate for Mr. Mongeau to continue to serve on the Norfolk Southern Board given the recent disclosures at TD Bank.

 

We have identified a pattern of high profile regulatory and compliance problems at companies where Mr. Mongeau has served as a director:

 

·Mr. Mongeau sat for over a decade on SNC-Lavalin Group Inc. board and audit committee, from 2003 to 2015.1 Between 2001 and 2011, a senior company executive admitted paying tens of millions of dollars of company funds in bribes to secure construction projects in Libya.2 Mr. Mongeau’s tenure on the SNC-Lavalin board overlapped in large part with the board’s oversight failures with respect to these illicit payments.

 

 

1 https://financialpost.com/news/fp-street/cns-claude-mongeau-joins-snc-lavalin-audit-committee

2 https://www.cbc.ca/news/business/snc-lavalin-trial-board-1.5096153

 

 

·We note that the SNC-Lavalin owns Atkins Nuclear Secured, the company hired by Norfolk Southern to perform a safety assessment after East Palestine – despite having no railroad industry experience.3

 

·Since 2015, Mr. Mongeau has served as a director of TD Bank. In 2023, Canadian financial regulator FinTRAC fined the company millions and “deemed its anti-money-laundering compliance to be unsatisfactory.”4

 

·This week’s news is even more troubling. The Wall Street Journal reported that the DOJ “investigation into TD Bank’s internal controls focuses on how Chinese crime groups and drug traffickers used the Canadian lender to launder money from U.S. fentanyl sales.”5 TD Bank disclosed three other anti-money laundering investigations and has set aside as an initial provision of $450 million for just one of those, noting it expects additional monetary penalties. FinTRAC also separately fined TD Bank again this week.6

 

·We note that Mr. Mongeau serves on the corporate governance committee, the responsibilities of which are to “[p]rovide oversight of enterprise-wide conduct risk and enterprise-wide complaints, and act as the conduct review committee for the Bank and certain of its Canadian subsidiaries that are federally regulated financial institutions.”7 TD Bank’s announcement this week said that its anti-money laundering “program was insufficient to effectively monitor, detect, report, and respond to suspicious activity.”

 

·Mr. Mongeau’s time with the TD Bank board has failed to result in the implementation of the rigorous oversight programs that were needed to prevent authorities in more than one country from pursuing multiple enforcement actions against the company. These board failures will apparently result in quite significant costs TD Bank’s shareholders.

 

In light of the newly disclosed regulatory concerns combined with the previous instances, we believe you should ask Mr. Mongeau to step down. Multiple regulators and government agencies are dedicating their focus to Norfolk Southern’s actions every day – the Board needs the contributions of directors with a track record of success in developing and overseeing compliance programs.

 

  Regards,
   
  /s/ Jim Chadwick
  James Chadwick  
  President
  Ancora Alternatives LLC
