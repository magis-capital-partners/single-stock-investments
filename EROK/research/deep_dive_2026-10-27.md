## What this business is

Cash was $61.77 million at 30 June 2026. Class A shares outstanding were 24,455,688 at 30 June 2026. On 13 August 2026 the cover shows 26,355,259 Class A shares and 105,164,311 Class B shares. ## Why the market might be wrong

A new listing with rising revenue can look like a royalty on Permian oil. The filing says the company does not own the oil and gas minerals. **[Fact]** Revenue is surface use, resource contracts, and royalties on activity that happens on the land, including related-party arrangements. **[Inference]** A buyer who pays an oil-royalty multiple for Class A shares alone is paying the wrong asset and the wrong share count. ## Business & moat

The land position is the business. About 286,000 acres, plus an interest in up to about 70,000 acres under a dedication. **[Fact]** A Delaware flow agreement takes 90% of net proceeds in the description used in the quarterly report's minimum-royalty section: a minimum annual royalty of $40.0 million for the first five years of the initial term and $10.0 million for the last five years. **[Fact]** That minimum is a contract term. It is not cash already received in a full year of public results.

The credit facility matures on 8 June 2031. **[Fact]** During the six months the company repaid $258.610 million of long-term debt. Predecessor long-term debt at 31 December 2025 was $300.667 million. **[Fact]** Those repayments belong to the IPO period. They are not a statement that debt is zero today. On 10 August 2026 an acquisition of about $78.2 million was funded with about $80 million drawn on the credit facility. **[Fact]** June 30 cash of $61.77 million is before that draw.

Share-based compensation of $61.438 million in six months is larger than six-month revenue is not. It is larger than operating cash, which was negative. A non-GAAP free cash flow line in the quarterly highlights is not used as owner earnings. The GAAP operating cash figure is the one in the evidence file.

### Mental models

Surface land can collect fees when other people drill, build pipes, or use water. That is a toll on activity, not a share of the oil. The Up-C means the public share is a slice of a larger unit count. Counting only Class A shares makes every per-share figure too large.

### Business mechanics

Customers pay for surface use and related royalties. Some of that is related party. The company then pays overhead, debt costs, and, in this first half, a large stock grant. Growth spending and acquisitions are extra. The August draw shows acquisitions are funded with the revolver, not only with cash on hand.

### Moat

The acres are where the drilling is. **[Inference]** Location is an advantage only while operators need that surface and the contracts keep the minimums. One public quarter does not prove the minimums arrive as cash. Moat is unproven.

## Risks & inversion

**Primary risk:** Revenue depends on Permian activity and on related-party surface contracts, while the public float is a minority of the units and the revolver was drawn after the quarter.

- Six-month operating cash flow was negative $2.166 million.
- Stock-based pay of $61.438 million will not repeat in that size every quarter, and it also means the share count is not finished moving.
- The company does not own the oil and gas minerals. A mineral-royalty comparison is the wrong asset.

**Lens failure mode:** Valuing Class A shares as if they were the whole company, or as if they owned the oil.

**Upside / downside from price:** Upside is the minimum royalties showing up as cash for a full year after the stock grant. Downside is activity slowing while the new debt is outstanding.
