## Natural collector transition

`reverse-split-shadow-warm` prepares readers in a separate GitHub runner, then waits at most 30 minutes for the existing bounded D1 collector lease. Standby holds no market-data or broker connection. The active owner dispatches a prepared successor ten minutes before its existing time limit; cron and ordinary continuation remain fallbacks. No active run is cancelled. A first transition from older software requires one owner-dispatched warm event near the old run's natural end.

The departing v2 runner drains background work while quotes remain active, commits its final checkpoint, closes its Databento helper and waits for confirmed process closure before releasing ownership. The successor atomically claims ownership, restores and verifies the original SQLite checksum, then opens its feed. Two contenders cannot both claim the lease. Missing ownership or failed helper shutdown fails closed.

R2 checkpoints use gzip level 1, with separate stored and original byte counts and SHA-256 checksums. Restore accepts old uncompressed receipts and bounds decompression. `collector-standby/latest.json`, runtime `pipeline_commit`, checkpoint receipts and validation samples establish actual transition timing. A local compression benchmark is not production latency evidence.

## Source recovery

An incomplete SEC reconciliation stays pending and retries unfinished days. Successfully archived daily indexes remain reusable. External readers accept bounded 96 MiB primary reports and 128 MiB relevant-document totals; the Worker and full-submission limits remain bounded. Original terminal failures are archived before a single versioned retry.

Publisher recovery uses unfiltered PRNewswire lists and the public GlobeNewswire date-filtered search inventory. Empty application shells, missing clocks, repeated pages and malformed inventories never establish coverage. It follows up to 120 pages per gap over bounded jobs, includes a minute overlap at both interval boundaries, and queues every interval article regardless of its headline. Closure requires both interval bounds and completed article jobs with original article archive checksums. Recovery proofs retain the original interval, page clocks/hashes, article clocks/hashes and late observation. Public pagination cannot prove deleted articles never existed. None of this recreates missed original-clock fills.

The Glaukos PDF supplement is limited to two visually inspected cover/logo pages in one exact 29-page file checksum. It accepts no OCR candidate and changes no financial value. Original extraction failures and the reviewed original are retained independently.

SEC prior-split searches now use ten-digit CIKs and include “reverse share split.” An actual Silexion replay changed an empty unpadded search to 65 matching documents across 37 distinct filing accessions; those are candidate filings, not 37 verified splits. Bounded 100-result pagination archives each source page, checks issuer/accession/date identity, and preserves partial status at the search ceiling. A versioned repair archives original job and issuer states before scheduling replacement searches. New announcements promote pending history work without resetting completed jobs. Only completed primary-source actions enter the verified prior-split ledger; late discoveries never create original-clock fills.

Initial financial-score latency is taken from the first immutable decision with a numeric score and at least three components. First analysis, first score and latest analysis remain separate clocks. A later rescore cannot replace initial timing, and an incomplete score never manufactures a ready timestamp.
