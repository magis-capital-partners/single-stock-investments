# Checkpoint integrity recovery — November 15, 2026

The collector stopped during the November 15 UTC handoff. Subsequent runs rejected
the compressed checkpoint because its R2 bytes differed from the committed D1
receipt. Both original slot files decompress successfully and pass SQLite
`quick_check`; neither fact alone authorizes ignoring a checksum mismatch.

The receipt expected 130,512,370 compressed bytes with SHA-256
`8244c233a21fe75058e730f253d09c814a86d5d218fbf81d62ec55a1515c0f4c`.
Slot 1 actually contained 130,512,749 bytes, SHA-256
`96e3719545e9d202dae0bb949080a274e68551aafba7b3921f4ad2476530f912`.
The two original objects and the original receipt are preserved separately from
the recovery pointer; their mismatch is not rewritten as a successful old save.

## Reproduction and fix

`node --test dashboard/cloudflare/test-reverse-split-checkpoint-recovery.mjs`
reproduced the exact production rejection in a deterministic late-upload test:
the previous owner uploads to a reused slot after the successor commits. The
previous owner's receipt update is rejected, but its upload has already replaced
the successor's bytes. The precise server-side completion order of the production
network failure cannot be reconstructed from the available logs.

Each new checkpoint now uses a non-reused generation UUID and its recorded byte
digest in the object key. Saves through a handle are serialized; receipt commits
compare the exact prior receipt and, in production, the live collector lease.
Stale writers cannot advance the pointer or overwrite another generation.
Checksum checks remain mandatory. Legacy slots remain readable and untouched.

Normal retention keeps the current and immediately previous committed generation.
Only older keys introduced in the checkpoint-generation namespace are pruned.
Legacy slots, immutable review/recovery archives and uncertain failed uploads are
not pruned. Failed/uncertain writes can leave forensic orphan objects; recovery
does not silently delete them or fall back to an older ledger and lose records.

## Recovery evidence

Both original slot objects were independently uploaded/downloaded and decompressed
under `review/recovery-inputs/2026-11-15/`, bound to their actual SHA-256 digests.
The selected slot 1 copy preserves every row from slot 0 except mutable operational
summaries and watch status. Their decisions, policies, histories, records, source
documents, first-observation clocks, borrow observations and quote archives agree.
It also preserves every immutable record and first-observation clock in the
independently verified November 11 archived ledger, including all 64 execution
policies. Frozen review SHA-256 remains
`7e97670dabb5d7ebd15e64891e4fce4e8cc938a233ea5e753f822f5768e15bc6`.

A recovery manifest must bind these comparisons and both original receipts to a
new, independently read-back generation. Publishing that reviewed recovery
pointer is separate from fixing the code. It requires an expired/unowned lease
and an unchanged original pointer. No original slot is overwritten. Restart uses
the existing repository continuation event and normal bounded lease acquisition.
Actual ownership, source progress, checkpoint hashes and publication must then be
verified; a merge or successful deployment alone is not recovery.
