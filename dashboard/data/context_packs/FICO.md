## What this business is (stable)
Fair Isaac sells a credit score and the software that uses scores in lending decisions. The three national credit bureaus, Experian, TransUnion, and Equifax, run the formula on their files and pay Fair Isaac a fee for each score. A smaller direct-to-consumer business sells scores at myFICO.com. The software segment covers fraud, origination, account management, and a subscription platform. In the year ended September 30, 2025, Scores were $1.17 billion and Software was $822 million.

## Why the market might be wrong (stable)
The September 29 selloff prices a break in mortgage score pricing power. That reading fits the last quarterly report, which said business-to-business score growth came mainly from a higher mortgage unit price. It does not fit a claim that the whole company just lost its customers. Classic FICO is still allowed at Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac, and it is still required when a loan is underwritten by hand. The market can be wrong in either direction: too fearful if lenders keep paying up for the familiar score, or not fearful enough if the bureaus, who own the cheaper rival and distribute FICO, steer volume away.
