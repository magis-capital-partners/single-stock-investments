## What this business is (stable)
Archer-Daniels-Midland (NYSE: ADM) is a global agricultural supply chain manager and processor. **[Fact]** The company originates, transports, stores, and processes crops (especially soybeans, corn, and wheat) and converts them into vegetable oils, meal, starches, sweeteners, and nutrition ingredients for food, feed, fuel, and industrial customers. **[Fact]** FY2025 revenue was **$80.3 billion**, down from **$85.5 billion** in 2024 and **$93.9 billion** at the 2023 cycle peak. **[Fact]** FY2025 net earnings were **$1.08 billion** (**$2.23** basic EPS) versus **$1.80 billion** (**$3.66** EPS) in 2024 and **$3.48 billion** (**$6.44** EPS) in 2023. **[Fact]** Operating cash flow rebounded to **$5.45 billion** in 2025 from **$2.79 billion** in 2024. **[Inference]** ADM earns when global crop flows, crush margins, and nutrition mix cooperate; earnings are cyclical even though the balance sheet and origination network are durable.

## Why the market might be wrong (stable)
The market may be treating ADM as a normalized **$6+ per share owner-cash compounder** after the 2025 cash-flow rebound, while underweighting earnings still roughly **40% below** the 2023 peak, **$5.6 billion long-term debt** against **$1.0 billion** cash, and **governance residue** from the intersegment accounting investigation (settled with the SEC in January 2026). **[Inference]** Q1 2026 EPS of **$0.62** on **$20.5 billion** revenue suggests stabilization, but crush-margin and China/trade volatility can compress owner cash quickly. The component contract prices a **-$7 per share** ag-cycle reserve for this reason; without it, headline owner-cash math overstates durability at **$85.67**.
