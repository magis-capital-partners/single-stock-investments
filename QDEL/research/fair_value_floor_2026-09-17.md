Point of care is seasonal and post-COVID. Leverage is the equity story: net debt about **$2.77 billion** against a **$540–560 million** FY2026 adjusted EBITDA guide.

## Floor method

A lower bound of fair value for a levered turnaround is **not** a 10 times sum-of-parts. The May/June SOTP of ~$49 used old EBITDA and peer-ish multiples. That is a *base or bull* picture if China normalizes and leverage falls. It is not a floor.

Rule used here:

1. Use the **low end** of current company EBITDA guidance as the trough run-rate. Do not haircut it further without a new print below the guide.
2. Apply **5.5 times** enterprise value, a step below the ~6 times the market already paid at the August print. Five times on the low end of the guide leaves **negative** equity, which is a liquidation statement, not a going-concern fair value.
3. Subtract **full** net debt. Zero for the unconfirmed point-of-care sale. Zero for LEX / molecular. Zero for 2026 free cash flow (guide withdrawn; H1 operating cash was negative).
4. Use Q2 basic shares.

## What would break the floor

- FY2026 adjusted EBITDA prints **below $540 million** for two consecutive quarters.
- Net debt rises (working-capital bleed, revolver draw, or a cash interest problem). H1 interest was **$106 million** on H1 adjusted EBITDA of **$238 million**.
- A going-concern or covenant event. Then the floor is the 5× case (~$0), not $4.

A confirmed point-of-care sale at cash proceeds of **$800 million or more**, applied to debt, would **raise** the floor. That is not in the number until an 8-K exists.
