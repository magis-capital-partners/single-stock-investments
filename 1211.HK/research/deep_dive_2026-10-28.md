## Business & moat

The business is a vertically integrated car and battery maker. The research note's reading of the filings is that scale and battery cost are a real advantage, and that the advantage is narrow because rivals and governments can take it away. [inference]

Trailing earnings per share are FY2025 CNY 3.58, minus first-half 2025 CNY 1.71, plus first-half 2026 CNY 1.35, which is CNY 3.22. [inference, research note] Times 1.176 is HKD 3.79. [inference] That is the owner-cash starting point. It assumes maintenance capital spending is about equal to depreciation, so owner earnings are taken as net income, and spending above depreciation is treated as growth spending. [Assumption] The note calls that generous: capital spending has not been below 1.5 times depreciation since FY2022, and bringing supplier payment days down still uses cash. [inference]

Free cash flow is not a usable owner-cash measure. Cumulative free cash flow from FY2016 through FY2025 was CNY 56.6 billion against CNY 140.3 billion of net income, and the positive years were largely funded by a rise in what the company owes suppliers. [inference] FY2025 free cash flow was negative CNY 97.7 billion. [inference, research note, from the cash flow statement]

Reported net cash looks mild (about CNY 10.8 billion at the end of FY2025 and about CNY 2.8 billion at 30 June 2026). [fact, research note, citing the balance sheets] At 30 June 2026, trade payables plus bills payable were about CNY 229.8 billion, roughly 132 days of trailing cost of goods. The excess over a 60-day norm was about CNY 125.1 billion, about HKD 16.10 per share. Adding perpetual bonds of CNY 20.85 billion takes the adjusted net debt to about CNY 143 billion, about HKD 18.50 per share. [inference] The model does not subtract either figure from the base-case owner cash. It shows both as a debt sensitivity in the valuation section. "Other payables" and contract liabilities are left out of that charge. [inference]

The moat is a cost moat, and it is under attack at home. In May 2025 BYD cut prices on 22 models by as much as 34%. [fact, research note, citing Caixin 27 May 2025 and Reuters] Beijing has since published a guide against selling below full cost (February 2026), and it has also cut into the profit pool: a battery consumption tax, a smaller export tax rebate from 1 April 2027, and a halved new-energy purchase tax from 1 January 2026. [fact, research note, citing the named official texts] The European Union's extra duty on BYD is 17% on top of the 10% ordinary duty. [fact, research note, citing Reuters 14 October 2024] On 9 June 2026 the U.S. Department of Defense added BYD to a list of Chinese military companies. BYD disputes that and says it is not a sanctions list. [fact, research note, citing the HKEX announcement and press] Return on equity was 3% to 12% in FY2016 through FY2021 and 15% to 25% in FY2022 through FY2025. [inference, research note table] That is a cyclical record, not a steady toll.

Berkshire Hathaway sold the H-share stake from August 2022 and carried it at zero by 31 March 2025. [fact, research note, citing HKEX disclosure-of-interest notices and Berkshire Hathaway Energy filings]

## Payoff & return

The payoff is owner cash from the operating business, not a sale of assets and not a sum of parts. There is no hard asset floor. The bear case is a negative annual return, so the downside test is not met. The base annual return is under 15%, and the cost advantage is being competed away at home, so the stance is watch. The house call is to wait.

**Upside / downside from price:** Upside that would change the call is a price in the HKD 32 to HKD 48 zone without a worse set of facts, or evidence that margins and cash both turn. Downside at HKD 70.45 is a base case of 7.7% a year, a bear case of -5.2% a year, and a worse result if excess supplier balances are treated as debt. The bull case is 16.5% a year. It is not the case this report underwrites. The valuation section has the arithmetic.

The price that earns 15% a year on the base path is HKD 48.29. After charging HKD 16.10 a share of excess supplier payables, that price is HKD 32.19. The house buy zone of HKD 32 to HKD 48 is those two figures, rounded to the nearest dollar. [inference, same cash-flow function as the valuation tool]

## Risks & inversion

**Primary risk:** The China price war keeps going, or starts again, and BYD keeps cutting price to hold share. That is the largest-player problem. The company's own May 2025 cuts are the exhibit. [fact, Caixin and Reuters as cited] [inference]

Other risks, in the order used in the 28 October 2026 note:

1. Supplier balances reverse. Excess trade and bills above 60 days were about CNY 125 billion. Bills payable rose sharply in the first half of 2026. [fact and inference, research note, citing the interim report]
2. The state cuts the profit pool. The export rebate steps down from 13% to 9% on 1 April 2027. The battery consumption tax steps up. The purchase-tax relief is already smaller. [fact, research note, citing the official announcements]
3. Trade walls. Europe's combined duty, a late Hungary plant, Turkey on hold, and the U.S. military-company listing. [fact, as cited above]
4. Capital spending stays near twice depreciation while cash flow is negative. FY2025 and the first half of 2026 both had negative free cash flow. [inference]
5. Accounting quality is not closed. The note flags capitalized research spending, guarantees, and a first-half foreign-exchange loss, some of them from secondary write-ups that were not re-checked against the PDF. [inference] [HUMAN REVIEW]
6. The 29 October 2026 release can move the starting earnings figure either way. Sell-side previews cited in the note are not used as the starting point. [fact, the previews exist in the note] [inference, they are not this case]

What confirms a pass at HKD 70.45: the price war continues, payment days do not settle, or capital spending keeps absorbing the earnings. What would reopen a buy: a price in the HKD 32 to HKD 48 zone with the facts no worse, or two quarters of a higher gross margin with steadier domestic prices, payment days held near 90 with operating cash at least equal to earnings, positive free cash flow at a normal capital-spending level, and the Hungary plant actually ramping. ## Valuation & IRR (assumption ledger)

This is a cash-flow case for an operating business. We start from cash the owner could take if maintenance spending matches depreciation, grow that cash for seven years, and assume a sale at a multiple of year-7 owner cash. The tool pays that owner cash each year and also applies the selling multiple in year 7. The price is in Hong Kong dollars. The accounts are in yuan. The starting figure is converted once, at 1.176 Hong Kong dollars per yuan, and then grown in Hong Kong dollars. A move in the yuan against the Hong Kong dollar goes straight into the starting figure. [inference]

The headline is the base case from `_system/scripts/marvin_valuation.py`. It is not a blend of the bear and bull cases. The tool's one-decimal annual returns match the supplied case: 7.7%, -5.2%, and 16.5%. The unrounded tool output was 7.6719%, -5.1918%, and 16.4770%.

#### IRR arithmetic (show your work)

How the annual return at today's price was calculated. Inputs are a price of HKD 70.45 and starting owner cash of HKD 3.79 per share. Years 1 through 5 grow at 8%. Years 6 and 7 grow at 5%. Year 7 also adds 12 times that year's owner cash. The cash amounts are the rounded stream from `marvin_valuation.cashflows_full`. Currency HKD.

1. Price paid today: HKD 70.45.
2. Year 1 owner cash: 3.79 times 1.08 = HKD 4.09.
3. Year 2: HKD 4.42.
4. Year 3: HKD 4.77.
5. Year 4: HKD 5.16.
6. Year 5: HKD 5.57.
7. Year 6, now at 5%: HKD 5.85.
8. Year 7 owner cash is inside the final receipt. Cash received in year 7, including 12 times that year's owner cash, is HKD 79.81.
9. The valuation tool solves the annual return that sets the present value of this stream equal to the price. That return is 7.7% per year. The bear stream (down 2% then flat, 8 times) is -5.2% per year. The bull stream (15% then 8%, 15 times) is 16.5% per year.

The price that earns 15% a year is the present value of the same future stream, using the tool's cash-flow function with a zero purchase price. On the base path that price is HKD 48.29. On the bear path it is HKD 25.10. On the bull path it is HKD 75.99. Those round to the supplied one-decimal figures of HKD 48.3, HKD 25.1, and HKD 76.0. The tool also stores a fallback present-value range on the operating business. That range is not the 15% entry price and it is not the buy zone.

Debt sensitivity, still on the base growth path. Counting HKD 16.10 a share of excess trade and bills as debt is the same as paying HKD 86.55 for the HKD 3.79 of owner cash. The tool's annual return is then 4.0%. The 15% price falls by HKD 16.10, to HKD 32.19. Counting the fuller HKD 18.50 (that excess plus perpetual bonds, about CNY 143 billion) does not reproduce 4.0% or HKD 32.2. At a price of HKD 88.95 the tool returns 3.5% a year (unrounded 3.521%). The 15% price less HKD 18.50 is HKD 29.79. The house buy zone stays HKD 32 to HKD 48, which is the HKD 16.10 version, rounded. The difference is reported. It is not forced back to 4.0%.

**Returns statement:** At HKD 70.45 per share, the base case is **7.7%** per year over seven years on starting owner cash of HKD 3.79 per share.
