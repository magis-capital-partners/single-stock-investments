Item 5.07. Submission of Matters to a Vote of Security Holders.

 

Fermi Inc., a Texas corporation (the “Company”), held its 2026 Annual Meeting of Shareholders (the “Annual Meeting”) on October 30, 2026. There were 641,259,703 shares issued and outstanding and entitled to vote as of August 31, 2026, the record date for the Annual Meeting.

 

The following are final voting results for the proposals considered and voted upon at the Annual Meeting, each of which is more fully described in the Company’s definitive proxy statement for the Annual Meeting filed with the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission on October 2, 2026:

 

Proposal 1 (Election of Two Class I Directors): Although each nominee received a majority of the votes cast, neither nominee received the affirmative vote of a majority of the shares outstanding and entitled to vote, as required by Section 2.8 of the Company’s Amended and Restated Bylaws (the “Bylaws”). As a result, neither nominee was elected to serve as a Class I director until the Company’s 2029 Annual Meeting of Shareholders. Under Section 5.2(d) of the Company’s Certificate of Formation and Section 3.2 of the Bylaws, however, each director holds office until his successor is duly elected and qualified. Accordingly, Governor Perry and Mr. Stein each continue to serve as Class I directors of the Company, with the same authority as any other director, and there is no vacancy on the Board of Directors. Votes were as follows:

Governor Perry Would Have Been Elected Had the Director Nomination Agreement Been Honored: Governor Perry was nominated as the designee of Caddis Capital, LLC (“Caddis”) under the Director Nomination Agreement, dated September 30, 2025, among the Company, TMNN Manager LLC, Caddis, and the Melissa A. Neugebauer 2020 Trust (the “MN Trust”) (the “DNA”). Section 2.3 of the DNA requires each investor party to the DNA, including TMNN Manager LLC and the MN Trust, entities affiliated with Toby Neugebauer, to vote its shares in favor of the election of the nominees designated under the DNA. Before the Annual Meeting, the Company notified those investors of that obligation and demanded that they vote for Governor Perry. Based on information available to the Company, the Company believes that those investors’ shares were instead voted against Governor Perry, in breach of the DNA. Those investors, through counsel, have disputed that the DNA required them to vote for Governor Perry.

 

The Company’s definitive proxy statement for the Annual Meeting reported that TMNN Manager LLC and the MN Trust together held 98,859,659 shares as of the record date. Adding those shares to the votes cast for Governor Perry reported above would result in 348,108,017 votes for his election, more than the 320,629,852 votes required for election.

 

The Company reserves all rights and remedies available to it under the DNA, at law, and in equity, including the right to seek an order giving effect to the votes the DNA required.
