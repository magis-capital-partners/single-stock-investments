## What this business is
AMREP Corporation (NYSE: AXR) is a Rio Rancho, New Mexico land developer and a small homebuilder. It owns about 16,200 acres in Sandoval County, sells developed lots and raw land to builders, and builds single-family homes itself. Fiscal 2026 revenue was $52.8 million. The company has 52 employees, almost no borrowed money, and a large cash balance. A defined-benefit pension and an old fulfillment business are no longer operating segments. The stock is a claim on cash, houses in process, and land carried at historical cost.

---

## Why the market might be wrong
The predictive attribute is acreage that the balance sheet still carries at cost, in a town where finished lots have sold for about $820,000 an acre and two bulk raw tracts sold near $4,600 an acre. The inefficiency is that a thin stock with three holders owning about half the shares is priced off recent earnings, which collapsed when developed-lot sales paused. At the October 22, 2026 close of $20.91, the equity is worth about $111 million against $141 million of book equity and $48.7 million of cash. That is a discount to book. It is not, on the filing split we can actually see, a price of zero for the raw acres. There is no hard catalyst. Cash and the absence of debt are the cushion while land sales stay episodic.

## Risks & inversion
**Primary risk:** The discount never closes inside a useful window. Management can hold the land, spend the cash on development or a purchase, and leave holders with overhead and no dividend. The 10-K says future growth, if pursued, requires buying more New Mexico land or entering other markets.

**Lens failure mode:** Treating one bulk sale, or the GAAP carrying value, as cash that arrives on a schedule. The asset lens fails when a single $4,600 print is applied to scattered acres the company itself says are not a guide, or when book equity is treated as downside protection.

- Customer concentration. All fiscal 2026 developed residential land sales were to three builders. A pause is already in the July quarter (`AXR/investor-documents/sec-edgar/10-K_20260724_rpt20260430_acc0001104659_26_086659.htm`).
- Affordability. The company cut prices, offered incentives, leased finished homes, and slowed starts. Q1 average price fell to $407 thousand (`AXR/investor-documents/sec-edgar/10-Q_20260911_rpt20260731_acc0001104659_26_107084.htm`).
- Control and liquidity. About 52% is held by three shareholders. Average volume was 13,210 shares a day into April 30, 2026. A low-premium insider transaction is a real path. The 2022 purchase was at a discount to the prior close, not a premium (`AXR/investor-documents/sec-edgar/DEF 14A_20230802_rpt20230907_acc0001104659_23_086818.htm`).
- Entitlement and infrastructure delays already pushed revenue out of fiscal 2026 and are cited as a reason fiscal 2027 land revenue should be lower.
- The federal loss carryforward can be limited by an ownership-change test. The filing states that possibility. It does not say a change has occurred.
- Water, tariffs, and labor are disclosed as cost and constraint risks. They are not quantified here.

#### IRR arithmetic (show your work)

How we calculated the annual return. No cash is paid along the way. The annual return is the rate that turns today's price into the case value in ten years.

1. Base value is $157.900 million, or $29.65 a share. Price is $20.91. The ratio is 29.65 / 20.91 = 1.418.
2. A ten-year compound rate on 1.418 is 1.418 to the power of one-tenth, minus one, which is 3.6% per year.
3. Bear value $20.15 against $20.91 is 0.964. Over ten years that is about -0.4% per year.
