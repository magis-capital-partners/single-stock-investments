# Volume residual on the volatility panel

**Date:** 2026-10-09
**Status:** built 2026-10-09. Formula, SPY sidecar, market card, and ticker line are in. The ticker line fills on the next technical refresh, because that job is what walks each name's daily volume.
**Source:** László Gillemot, J. Doyne Farmer, and Fabrizio Lillo, *There's More to Volatility than Volume*, SFI working paper 2005-12-041.

**Surface:** the volatility panel already drawn on the market-risk page by `dashboard/vol-viz.js` (`VolViz.renderVolPanel`), plus one line on the open ticker inside the technical panel. No new tab. No new route.

## 0. What this is

Close-to-close volatility mixes two things. Some days are busy and some are quiet, so a fixed calendar window sometimes covers a lot of shares and sometimes covers few. The paper measured returns on New York and London ticks after a fixed amount of volume, or a fixed number of trades, had gone by. Clustering and heavy tails were still there. Volume explained only a slice.

We cannot split a day. Yahoo daily bars are one close and one volume. The build is the daily cousin of that measurement, and the caption says so. It is not a reconstruction of the paper's tick result.

Three numbers, on the same instrument, in the same units (annualized volatility, in points, so 16 means 16%):

1. **Calendar-time volatility.** How much the adjusted close moved across the last 20 sessions.
2. **Volume-time volatility.** The same closes, recounted only after a fixed number of shares has traded.
3. **The gap.** Calendar minus volume-time. This gap, dated, is the series.

The gap is a description of the history. It is not a forecast, not a regime call, and not an input to a price or a return.

Schema on every payload: `research_only: true`, `in_base_irr: false`, `authority: "none"`.

## 2. The market instrument is SPY

`spx_rv20` in `build_vol_metrics.py` is the 20-day close-to-close realized volatility of `^GSPC`, annualized by the square root of 252, in vol points. That number stays where it is. It is the index.

The index does not trade shares. Yahoo's volume on `^GSPC` is not a share count we can use as a clock. The market residual uses **SPY**: adjusted close for the return, and the share volume Yahoo already returns on the daily chart. Calendar volatility and volume-time volatility are both computed on SPY, so the gap is one instrument minus itself.

The card may print the existing SPX 20-day number beside SPY's calendar number, labeled as the index. They will not match. The gap is never SPX minus SPY, and never VIX minus anything.

## 3. The formula

One module, no network and no file reads: `_system/scripts/volume_residual.py`. Both builders import it. The volatility panel and the ticker line cannot grow two different definitions.

### 3.1 Calendar-time volatility

Match the existing window.

- Price is the adjusted close.
- Daily return is the log of today's adjusted close over yesterday's.
- On date t, take the last 20 finite daily returns ending at t. That is `RV_WINDOW = 20` in `build_vol_metrics.py`.
- Calendar volatility is the sample standard deviation of those 20 returns (divide by 19) times the square root of 252, times 100.
- Fewer than 20 finite returns: the date is withheld. Do not fill a missing close.

### 3.2 The volume quota

The quota is the median daily share volume over the 252 sessions strictly before the bucket opens. Today's volume does not set today's quota. A later day does not revise an earlier quota.

Median, not mean. One huge print should not set the size of every bucket that year.

The quota is frozen when a bucket opens. It does not change while shares are still accumulating.

### 3.3 Equal-volume buckets

Walk the history in date order.

- Start a bucket at the previous close.
- Add each day's share volume.
- When accumulated volume reaches the quota that was frozen at the open, close the bucket.
- The bucket's return is the log of the closing adjusted price over the adjusted price at the bucket's start.
- Reset and open the next bucket on the next session.

A day whose volume is already at least the quota is one bucket by itself. Its return is that day's log return. **A daily bar is never split.** That is the whole difference from the paper, and the caption repeats it.

A quiet stretch may take many sessions to fill one bucket. That is the point of the clock.

Record, on each bucket, the end date, the return, the share volume actually accumulated, the number of sessions inside it, and whether the bucket was a single day that overshot the quota.

### 3.4 Volume-time volatility

On date t, take the last 20 buckets whose end date is on or before t.

Volume-time volatility is the sample standard deviation of those 20 bucket returns, times the square root of the number of such buckets in a year, times 100.

The number of buckets in a year is the mean daily volume over the same prior 252 sessions, times 252, divided by the quota. Mean for the count, median for the quota. They are not the same number, and the payload stores both.

Fewer than 20 completed buckets: withhold the volume-time number and the gap. Calendar volatility may still print.

### 3.5 The gap

Gap = calendar volatility minus volume-time volatility, in vol points.

Also store the ratio of volume-time to calendar, so a change in the level of volatility is not mistaken for a change in the gap. The card's headline series is the gap. The ratio sits in the disclosure, not in the three big figures.

A positive gap means close-to-close movement is larger than the equal-volume recount. A negative gap means the opposite. Neither sign is a signal.

### 3.6 What a clean case should do

These are the locked tests, not the live numbers.

- Constant volume and steady returns: the gap is near zero. Sampling noise remains. The test uses a seeded series and a tolerance, not an exact zero.
- Movement that lives only on busy days, with quiet days nearly flat: volume-time volatility absorbs most of it, and the absolute gap is smaller than the calendar number.
- Movement that alternates in size with no relation to volume: a gap remains.
- A day with volume above the quota is one bucket. The test counts the buckets and asserts the day was not divided.
- The quota on a date does not change if later volumes are appended.

## 4. When the clock is too coarse

Withhold the gap, and say why in one short clause, when any of these is true:

- Fewer than 20 finite daily returns.
- Fewer than 20 completed buckets.
- More than one in five buckets in the window is a single day that overshot the quota. The clock is then mostly just the calendar, and the gap would be fake precision.
- Volume is missing on more than one in twenty sessions in the lookback used to build those buckets.

A withheld ticker shows no line. The market card still shows calendar volatility and the clause. It does not show a zero gap.

Do not drop a split day by guessing. Yahoo's chart volume is the series `fetch_yahoo_history` already stores. If a session has no volume, or volume is zero, it does not advance the clock and it does not emit a bucket. The return across that hole is not invented.

## 5. The series

For SPY, write one row per completed session after the warmup, strictly trailing, same rule as the vol-metrics z-scores: a row's numbers use only data up to that row.

Fields:

- date
- calendar volatility
- volume-time volatility
- gap
- ratio
- quota (shares)
- mean daily volume used in the annualization
- buckets in the window
- share of buckets that were a single oversized day
- withheld reason, or null

File: `dashboard/data/vol_volume_residual.json`.

Do not add these fields to `vol_metrics_history.jsonl`. That file is the VIX complex. Mixing a SPY volume clock into every row couples two builds and ships extra weight on every risk-page load. The residual file holds the SPY history (the panel draws 120 sessions of the gap) plus the latest point.

The latest point also carries:

- as of
- price source (Yahoo symbol SPY, adjusted close, share volume)
- the caption string, stored with the data so the page cannot drift from the build
- `research_only`, `in_base_irr`, `authority`

Ticker latest points do not go in that file. They go on the existing technical summary, under the ticker, as `volume_residual` with the same fields for the latest date only, plus a withheld reason. The technical builder already walks daily history per name. It calls the same module. No second Yahoo client and no second definition.

A ticker with no usable volume gets `withheld_reason` and the panel omits the line.

## 6. The card

Market card, under the regime tiles, above the z-score heatmap.

Three figures, in this order: calendar-time volatility, volume-time volatility, gap. One unit label: annualized, in vol points.

Under them, the gap over the last 120 sessions as a simple strip. No z-score coloring. A z-score would turn the gap into a rarity score, and this panel already uses z-scores for VIX and its cousins. The strip is the level of the gap, nothing else.

Caption, in full, on the market card:

> Calendar-time volatility is how much SPY's adjusted close moved over the last 20 sessions. Volume-time volatility recounts those closes only after a fixed number of shares has traded. The gap is what remains after busy and quiet days are given equal weight. It is not a forecast. These are daily bars. The paper measured New York and London ticks, which can split one day into many slices. A daily bar cannot, so a single busy day is one bucket, never a fraction of one.

One shorter line on the ticker, only when the gap is present:

> {Name}: calendar {X}, equal-volume {Y}, gap {Z}. Daily bars, not a tick clock, and not a forecast.

The index's existing 20-day number may appear as a footnote on the market card: "SPX 20-day realized is {spx_rv20}. That is the index. The gap above is SPY against itself."

Disclosure, closed by default: quota, mean volume, how many buckets were a single oversized day, and the lag-1 autocorrelation of absolute daily returns next to the lag-1 autocorrelation of absolute bucket returns, each over the trailing year of its own clock. If both are positive, one sentence: absolute moves are still clumped after the equal-volume packing. If either cannot be computed, omit the sentence. This is the paper's clustering remark. It is not a second headline.

## 7. Copy that does not appear

Do not write forecast, signal, target weight, buy, sell, fair value, IRR, or a suggested scale. Do not color the gap green or red. Do not say the gap is cheap or crowded. Do not call a large gap a regime.

The existing vol-panel footer stays. This card does not get its own authority sentence beyond the caption.

## 8. Wiring

- `build_vol_metrics.py` fetches SPY with the Yahoo chart call it already uses for indexes, passes adjusted close and volume into `volume_residual.py`, and writes `dashboard/data/vol_volume_residual.json`. A failed SPY fetch leaves the previous file in place and stamps the card stale. It does not blank the VIX history.
- `build_technical_signals.py` calls the same function on the daily rows it already has and stores the latest point on each ticker. Names that fail the withhold rules store the reason and no gap.
- `dashboard/vol-viz.js` gains one render function for the card. `renderVolPanel` places it after the regime tiles. Missing file: one sentence that the residual is not loaded, and the rest of the panel draws as it does today.
- The technical panel reads `volume_residual` off the ticker payload. No extra request.
- `dashboard/index.html` loads the residual JSON next to `vol_metrics_latest.json`, the same way the other vol feeds fail soft.

Cache-bust the vol script the way the holder line did, only when the file actually changes.

## 11. Out of scope

- Tick data, trade counts, or a transaction-time clock. We do not have prints inside the day.
- A tail exponent. It is easy to overread and it is not the series we promised.
- Putting the gap on the z-score heatmap or into the term-structure tile.
- A per-ticker history strip. The ticker gets the latest three numbers. SPY gets the strip.
- Precomputing all 846 names into the risk-page payload. The ticker point rides the technical summary that already loads with the holdings page.
- Any write into valuation, Darwin, the holder map, or the time-average panel.
