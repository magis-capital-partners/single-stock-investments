## 1. Bottom line
- **Intel is now two businesses. One is getting much better: server CPUs. One is still a hole: the foundry. The price assumes the hole fills.**
- **Revenue [F→I]:**
  - Data Center and AI grew 78% to $7.3B. That growth is all of Intel's growth and more.
  - PC chips fell 5%. Reuters reports the CFO expects PC revenue to fall "mid-twenty" percent in Q4, with weakness through H1 2027 (memory shortages are squeezing PC makers).
  - The Israel fab explosion costs about $600M of revenue over two quarters.
  - So next quarter's revenue goes *down* ($15.0–16.0B guide vs $16.5B), well below the $17.1B consensus.
  - Server-CPU demand from "agentic AI" looks real and lasting. Intel says industry server CPU units grow 30%+ in both 2026 and 2027.
- **Margins [F→I]:**
  - Gross margin was 44.1% (non-GAAP), up 4.1 points. The foundry loss shrank to 27.7% of segment revenue (from 54.8%).
  - The guide is for gross margin to drop back to 38.0% in Q4 (about 2 points of that is Israel, per Reuters).
  - Structurally, margins are improving, but owner cash is nowhere near old Intel. Its best year in FY2017–2021, FY2020, was about $19.7B (OCF $35.9B − capex $14.3B − SBC $1.9B, SEC XBRL 10-K).
- **Owner earnings [F→I]:**
  - Trailing owner cash is about **$0.25 per share** (OCF $17.5B − gross capex $13.7B − SBC $2.4B, over 5.31B shares).
  - Capex is guided to ~$20B in 2026 and "significantly" higher in 2027. Owner cash likely turns negative next year.
  - Share count is up 17% year on year (government, Nvidia and SoftBank deals, plus the $23B offering).
  - So per-share owner earnings are being diluted while the build-out happens.
- **Thesis: stronger as a business than a year ago, unchanged-to-weaker as an investment.** The product side improved. The foundry is still unproven: there is no named external 14A volume customer. The Q4 guide shows how cyclical and supply-bound the near term is.
- **IRR at $92.07 (close 2026-10-29): base –9.9%** vs the 15% hurdle (bear –29.7%, bull +5.9%). The base-case 15% price is about $16. Even the bull case needs about $52. Verdict: **pass.** This is not a compounder at this price. It is a turnaround priced as a success.

**Funding history (the context for owner-earnings dilution) [F]:**
- **US government, Aug 2025:** 433.3M shares at $20.47 ($8.9B), a ~9.9% stake. Some shares sit in escrow and are released as Intel performs on the CHIPS "Secure Enclave" deal. Their fair value runs through GAAP P&L: –$12.5B in Q2'26, +$1.0B in Q3'26.
- **SoftBank, Aug 2025:** $2B at $23.
- **Nvidia, Sep 2025:** $5B at $23.28, plus a co-development deal (Reuters).
- **Apollo:** Intel bought back Apollo's 49% stake in the Ireland fab JV for $14.2B in April 2026 (Reuters). That explains most of the $14.4B of 9M-26 "partner distributions".
- **Aug 2026:** a $23B public offering at $95/share (StockTitan; release says "completed a $23 billion public equity offering").
- **Brookfield** still co-owns Arizona (SCIP).

**18A / 14A and foundry customers [F]:**
- 18A is in high-volume production in Arizona. Intel is still the only major customer (CNBC).
- The deck cites improving Intel 3 and 18A yields, with 18A-P coming in Q4.
- The Intel 14A 0.9 PDK (design kit) was released in October, on the schedule promised in July.
- 14A risk production is planned for H2 2027 and high volume for 2028 (Q2 call).
- **No named external 14A volume customer** (CNBC; prediction-market previews expected none).
- EMIB-T packaging yield and reliability are on target (deck).
- Ohio fab: first structural truss installed (CNBC). The next Arizona fab is expected by the end of 2028.

## 3. What management said that matters
- Tan (release): "Our immediate priority is to unlock additional supply to meet extraordinary demand for our Xeon family."
- Zinsner (release): "we see the PC slowdown continuing at least through the first half of next year."
- Zinsner (call, via CNBC): "We now expect industry server CPU units to grow more than 30% in both 2026 and 2027."
- Zinsner (call, via CNBC): "we will not spend $1 of capital unless we have confidence in the health of our products and process technologies, and clear evidence of customer demand."
- Zinsner (Reuters interview): Israel costs ~$300M of Q4 revenue and ~200 bp of Q4 gross margin, with a similar revenue hit in Q1. "We're still assessing all of the wafers..."

[I] The capital-discipline line is the right one for owners. But 2027 capex rising "significantly" before a named 14A customer shows the bet is still being placed ahead of proof.

**Red lines (for anyone holding, and for revisiting a pass):**
1. No signed external 14A volume customer by the end of Q2 2027 (risk production is H2 2027).
2. Foundry operating loss above $1.5B a quarter through 2027.
3. Non-GAAP gross margin below 38% for two quarters after Israel recovers (Q2 2027).
4. Another equity raise or convertible before 14A volume.
5. DCAI growth below 20% YoY while capex is still rising.

**What would prove this view wrong (dated):**
- A named, contracted 14A anchor customer (e.g. a large fabless or hyperscaler) with volume and prepayment, by mid-2027.
- Foundry breakeven in an actual quarter of 2027.
- Non-GAAP gross margin back above 45% in H2 2027.

If all three happen, the bull column becomes the base. Even then, the IRR at today's price is about 6%.

## 6. Lens views (short)
- **Buffett:**
  - Fails "absence of change" (Q10) and the fast-changing-tech avoid.
  - Serial share issuance (Q14/Q17: "one of the surest indicators of a promotion-minded management" — here driven by need, not promotion, but the per-share effect is the same).
  - Growth needs capital in proportion to earnings (Q11: "gruesome" risk). Too hard.
- **Munger:**
  - Q18 cash-take-out test: this is the "semis-type compulsory reinvestment" case he named (DJCO 2023).
  - Invert: Intel most likely disappoints by spending $20B+ a year on fabs that TSMC's customers never fill.
  - Too-hard pile.
- **Hohn:** the short-attribute avoid screen (2012 Q4) shows three of five: structurally declining business (PC), technology risk (foundry), high valuation. Leverage is fine and accounting is noisy but disclosed. ≥3 means **avoid** as a long. TCI's own semis bet was TSMC, "everyone's foundry" (2020 Q3 note), and that is the incumbent Intel must beat.
- **Pabrai:** no Dhandho. Heads it is a good business at a full price; tails you lose a lot. Fails the "why is it cheap?" question because it is not cheap (up ~150% YTD per CNBC). Leverage is fine.
- **Li Lu:** is it cheap? No. Is the moat getting stronger? In servers, maybe. In foundry, not yet. Is management trustworthy? Tan is credible on discipline (Zinsner's "$1 of capital" line). Why does the opportunity exist? It doesn't; this is the AI-index trade.
- **Stahl / Horizon Kinetics (context only):** Intel is a *provider* of scarce compute, which the lens likes. But it is the opposite of asset-light, and it sits in Stahl's own Devil's-Advocate list (INTC, with MSFT, GLW and MU, priced so that "success ≠ satisfactory return", HK 2024Q1). The Absurdity Nullification test: grant the bull premises and the bull IRR is still ~6%. **The thesis fails the test.**
- **Begg (twin lights):** business quality is improving. Investment quality is poor. "Mispriced does not mean cheap."

**Red-team (PRINCIPLES §F):**
- Kill case for "pass": TS16 Micron. Pabrai's cyclical bet looked bad on trough owner cash and then went up 15× on AI memory demand. If agentic AI makes x86 server CPUs a scarce, priced-up product and 14A lands, trailing-based IRRs badly understate Intel.
- Answer: the bull case already assumes peak-era owner cash and still clears only ~6%. The price, not the business, drives the pass.
